ESTABLISHED 1895

The Indian Army

The Indian Army is the land warfare branch of the armed forces, one of the largest standing armies in the world. It traces its lineage to the Presidency armies of British India and was reorganised into a unified force after independence in 1947. It is organised into seven operational commands plus the Army Training Command, each responsible for a defined geographic sector, and fields formations ranging from strike corps to mountain divisions built for high-altitude warfare.

ESTABLISHED 1612 (LINEAGE) / 1950 (REPUBLIC)

The Indian Navy

The Indian Navy safeguards the country's maritime borders and interests across the Indian Ocean Region. It operates through three commands — Western, Eastern and Southern — and fields a blue-water fleet built around two active aircraft carriers, nuclear and conventional submarines, and a growing indigenous shipbuilding programme.

ESTABLISHED 8 OCTOBER 1932

The Indian Air Force

The IAF secures Indian airspace and conducts aerial warfare, with a motto drawn from the Bhagavad Gita: "Touch the Sky with Glory." It is organised into seven commands spanning air defence, offensive strike, training and maintenance, and operates a mixed fleet of indigenous and imported fighters, transports and helicopters.

SEVEN DECADES OF SERVICE

A Short Military Timeline

A condensed timeline — not exhaustive — marking turning points since independence.

1947–48

First Kashmir War

The newly independent Indian Army airlifts troops into Srinagar to repel raiders, its first major operation.

1961

Operation Vijay (Goa)

Indian forces end Portuguese colonial rule over Goa, Daman and Diu.

1965

Second India-Pakistan War

Large tank battles in the Punjab and Sindh sectors, including the Battle of Asal Uttar.

1971

Bangladesh Liberation War

A coordinated tri-service campaign leads to the surrender of Pakistani forces in Dhaka and the creation of Bangladesh.

1999

Kargil War

Operation Vijay recaptures high-altitude posts in Kargil, with heavy IAF close air support under Operation Safed Sagar.

2019

Balakot Air Strike

IAF Mirage 2000 jets conduct a cross-border strike, followed by an aerial engagement over Kashmir.

2020

Galwan Valley Clash

A deadly close-quarter clash with Chinese forces in Ladakh triggers a major standoff and force build-up.

2022

INS Vikrant Commissioned

India's first indigenously built aircraft carrier enters service, a milestone for domestic shipbuilding.

TRI-SERVICE SPECIAL OPERATIONS

Special Forces Command Centre

India's premier special operations units, one from each service.

PARA (Special Forces)

Balidan — "Sacrifice"
  • Raised from the Parachute Regiment; battalions numbered from the 1 Para (SF) lineage.
  • Trained for direct action, deep reconnaissance and counter-terror operations.
  • Selection is famously attritional, run as a multi-week probation before permanent posting.

MARCOS

"A Frogman is Never Off Duty"
  • Marine Commando Force specialising in amphibious raids, ship-boarding and underwater demolition.
  • Draws volunteers from across the Navy through an extremely selective probation cycle.
  • Operates alongside the Navy's clandestine diving and special warfare units.

Garud Commando Force

"Swift and Bold"
  • Raised in 2004 to protect air bases and conduct combat search-and-rescue.
  • Trains jointly with Army and Navy special forces for joint special operations.
  • Increasingly employed in offensive special-operations roles alongside airbase defence.

HONOURING VALOUR

Gallantry Awards

Param Vir Chakra

Highest wartime award

India's highest military decoration for conspicuous bravery in the presence of the enemy — awarded 21 times since 1950.

Maha Vir Chakra

Second-highest wartime award

Awarded for acts of conspicuous gallantry in the presence of the enemy, on land, sea or air.

Vir Chakra

Third-highest wartime award

Recognises gallantry in battle, ranking just below the Maha Vir Chakra.

Ashoka Chakra

Highest peacetime award

The peacetime equivalent of the Param Vir Chakra, for the most conspicuous bravery outside war.

COMMON QUESTIONS

FAQ

What is the minimum age to join the NDA?+
Candidates are generally eligible between 16.5 and 19.5 years of age at the time of the exam; check the current UPSC notification for exact cut-offs.
What is the Agnipath scheme?+
Agnipath is a short-service enlistment model introduced in 2022 across the Army, Navy and Air Force, under which recruits called Agniveers serve an initial tour before a fraction are offered permanent retention.
Can women join the Indian Armed Forces?+
Yes — women are commissioned across most Army, Navy and Air Force branches, including permanent commission and, in recent years, combat roles such as fighter piloting.
How is NCC different from joining the Armed Forces directly?+
The National Cadet Corps is a youth training programme run in schools and colleges; it builds discipline and offers entry-exam advantages, but is not itself a military job — direct entry still requires schemes such as NDA, CDS or Agnipath.
